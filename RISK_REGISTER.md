# Risk Register

Scales: probability and impact are Low, Medium, High, or Critical. “Owner” names a role until people are assigned.

| ID | Risk | Probability | Impact | Mitigation / evidence required | Owner | Trigger or fallback |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|---|---|
| R-001 | The complete Android Auto head-unit protocol is not publicly documented. | High | Critical | Protocol provenance policy; legal review; P0/P1/P2/PX matrix; source assessment; written provenance questions for any GPL candidate; feasibility gate before session implementation. | Protocol lead | If no lawful maintainable source exists at M2, stop, pursue an official partner route, or approve a legally reviewed clean-room process. |
| R-002 | A phone/Android Auto update breaks reverse-engineered interoperability. | High | High | Phone/version matrix, nightly physical tests where feasible, strict version handling, redacted traces, fast rollback releases. | Protocol lead | Mark affected versions unsupported; never patch by unexplained constants. |
| R-003 | GPL or other source provenance forces a license incompatible with project intentions. | Medium | Critical | License inventory before copying any definition/code; default to GPL-compatible strategy if AASDK-derived work is adopted; retain notices/source obligations. | Maintainer/legal | Clean-room reimplementation or change project license/scope. |
| R-004 | Trademark/certification claims create legal or user-trust issues. | Medium | High | Neutral project name, trademark notice, no Google logos, explicit uncertified/R&D wording, counsel review. | Product/legal | Rename/rebrand and remove misleading distribution assets. |
| R-005 | Pi 5/CM5 does not officially advertise hardware H.264 decode while the phone may require H.264. | High | High | Runtime codec probe; benchmark software decode; negotiate lower mode when supported; investigate approved HEVC negotiation only from evidence. | Media lead | Support 720p30 software decode; state limitations or fail clearly if target is not met. |
| R-006 | “Zero-copy” is unavailable across decoder, GStreamer, Wayland, and GTK combinations. | Medium | High | M3 buffer-path instrumentation; accept bounded copies if latency budget passes; isolate adapter. | Media lead | Replace sink/integration or use a direct render adapter behind the same interfaces. |
| R-007 | Audio roles, focus, ducking, echo, or microphone timing behave incorrectly. | Medium | High | Typed audio roles, loopback tests, clock/jitter metrics, explicit focus state machine, reference USB audio hardware. | Media lead | Disable unsupported input/focus features and show degraded state. |
| R-008 | CM carrier USB host/device topology differs or is inadequately powered. | High | High | Preflight topology/power checks; reference carriers; powered hub guidance; never infer CM behavior from Pi 4/5 alone. | Platform lead | Declare carrier unsupported until its profile and wiring are validated. |
| R-009 | Touch rotation/scaling/letterboxing causes unsafe or inaccurate input. | Medium | High | Single coordinate transform, property tests, calibration UI, visual target test, reference display profiles. | UI lead | Disable projection input until calibration passes. |
| R-010 | Appliance graphical session conflicts with systemd security and device ownership. | Medium | High | Prototype dedicated user + compositor + DRM/input/audio access at M3; least-privilege audit; recovery console. | Platform/packaging | Offer desktop-session mode temporarily; do not run as root. |
| R-011 | Trixie library/plugin changes make pipelines vary across OS updates. | Medium | Medium | Pin tested minimum/maximum where necessary, capability probe, package integration tests against update snapshots. | Release lead | Hold affected dependency or publish a compatibility advisory. |
| R-012 | Native Rust bindings wrap C libraries with callback/threading hazards. | Medium | High | Isolate unsafe FFI, callback ownership tests, sanitizer runs where possible, dependency review. | Rust/media leads | Replace adapter/library; keep unsafe code out of domain crates. |
| R-013 | USB/session parsers process hostile or malformed phone data. | Medium | Critical | Size/depth/time limits, fuzzing, no panics, unprivileged runtime, current crypto libraries, security reporting process. | Security lead | Terminate session, publish advisory/fix, revoke affected release. |
| R-014 | Logs or fixtures leak contacts, voice, navigation, or device identifiers. | Medium | Critical | Redaction-by-construction, no payload logging, opt-in capture with warning/expiry, fixture provenance review. | Security/privacy | Remove artifacts/history as feasible, rotate release, notify affected users. |
| R-015 | Fast-boot work makes the device difficult to recover or update. | Medium | High | Optimize after measurement; preserve console/SSH recovery path; reversible config; package-owned changes only. | Platform lead | Restore standard boot target and document rollback. |
| R-016 | SD-card corruption or flash wear from abrupt vehicle power loss. | High | High | Atomic/rate-limited writes, minimal mutable state, journald limits, eMMC recommendation for CM, power-loss testing, hardware shutdown-controller guidance. | Platform lead | Read-only/degraded operation and state reset; recommend supported power hardware. |
| R-017 | Automotive electrical noise, brownouts, thermal limits, or poor grounding cause instability. | Medium | High | Reference regulated supply, cooling, USB power budget, undervoltage/thermal diagnostics, bench and in-vehicle validation disclaimers. | Hardware integrator | Degrade performance/show fault; do not treat software retries as a power fix. |
| R-018 | `.deb` installation scripts alter system/boot configuration unexpectedly. | Low | High | Minimal maintainer scripts, conffiles, explicit opt-in setup, install/upgrade/purge tests, no silent `/boot/firmware` edits. | Packaging lead | Roll back package/config and publish corrective package. |
| R-019 | GitHub-hosted CI cannot test real phones and Pi media hardware. | High | Medium | Layered fake/synthetic tests plus maintained physical runners; publish redacted hardware results. | Release lead | Block hardware-dependent releases when the lab is unavailable. |
| R-020 | Scope expands into wireless, CAN, radio, or general infotainment before wired stability. | Medium | High | PRD non-goals, milestone gates, issue labels, wired 1.0 before wireless implementation. | Product owner | Defer or split into a separate proposal. |
| R-021 | Use while driving creates distraction or liability risk. | Medium | Critical | No safety-critical controls; minimal local UI; follow applicable design/legal guidance; prominent experimental disclaimer; bench testing first. | Product/legal | Restrict distribution/use case if review finds unacceptable risk. |
| R-022 | Exact boot/latency goals fail on low-memory or thermally limited SKUs. | Medium | Medium | Test low-memory reference SKUs, publish measured tiers, tune negotiated resolution, thermal soak. | Performance lead | Raise minimum RAM/cooling requirements or lower media target transparently. |
| R-023 | The software assumes all CM4/CM5 modules include Wi-Fi/Bluetooth, or mistakes disabled/broken radios for absent hardware. | High | High | Independent runtime probes through kernel/udev, NetworkManager, BlueZ, rfkill, and firmware state; four-state result; non-wireless module tests. | Platform lead | Keep wired mode active and show an actionable wireless status instead of failing startup. |
| R-024 | The selected onboard or USB Wi-Fi cannot provide the required wireless Android Auto topology or coexist reliably with Bluetooth. | Medium | High | M8 AP/client and Bluetooth coexistence tests for each provider combination, regulatory-domain validation, signal/latency metrics, approved protocol evidence. | Wireless lead | Select another supported provider or keep wireless unsupported while preserving wired operation. |
| R-025 | A single custom carrier assumes CM4 and CM5 are fully interchangeable and connects a changed/reserved interface incorrectly. | Medium | Critical | Pin-by-pin matrix from both datasheets and transition guide, schematic peer review, per-module bring-up, straps/muxes where justified. | Hardware lead | Produce assembly variants or separate revisions if a safe common design cannot meet requirements. |
| R-026 | Custom carrier RF layout, metal enclosure, or antenna selection makes onboard Wi-Fi/Bluetooth unreliable. | Medium | High | Respect antenna keep-out, prefer approved external antenna path when required, RF pre-scan and enclosure testing on both modules. | Hardware/RF lead | Revise antenna/enclosure or require the external antenna configuration. |
| R-027 | Custom vehicle power/USB design damages a phone, module, or carrier during brownout/surge/role changes. | Medium | Critical | Qualified automotive power design review, USB current/role protection, ESD/surge/load-dump analysis, current-limited prototypes. | Hardware lead | Do not install in vehicle until bench compliance evidence exists. |
| R-028 | A USB radio is recognized but lacks required AP mode, Bluetooth features, maintained firmware, or stable drivers. | High | High | Publish a chipset/USB-ID compatibility list; probe capabilities; test on the supported OS/kernel; avoid marketing-name-only matching. | Wireless lead | Reject the adapter with an actionable diagnostic and recommend a tested model. |
| R-029 | USB phone, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, audio, and touchscreen devices exceed carrier hub bandwidth or power budget. | Medium | Critical | Per-carrier USB topology/current budget, powered and protected hub design, port-path binding, simultaneous-load testing. | Hardware/platform leads | Disable wireless startup or require a powered/reference adapter arrangement until hardware is corrected. |
| R-030 | Interface enumeration order changes, causing the wrong external radio to be selected. | Medium | High | Bind providers using stable physical port path and USB identity; expose selection in diagnostics; test reboot/hotplug order changes. | Platform lead | Require explicit administrator selection when identity is ambiguous. |
| R-031 | The Waveshare CM4 carrier's USB 2.0 hub, two exposed ports, or 5 V power budget is insufficient for phone, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, and USB audio simultaneously. | High | High | Map the hub and FFC expansion; measure simultaneous current/bandwidth; use a powered hub during development; design adequate protected hub ports on the custom PCB. | Hardware/platform leads | Reduce peripherals for a test or require the powered reference hub; never accept unstable brownout behavior. |
| R-032 | The original 800 × 480 display hides layout/scaling problems that appear on a larger thin-bezel display. | High | Medium | Resolution-independent UI, content-rectangle tests, multiple aspect-ratio simulations, and a second physical display before beta. | UI lead | Limit a release to tested display profiles while retaining configurable generic mode. |
| R-033 | NVMe boot/root setup consumes time before it adds value to the USB milestone. | Medium | Low | Use the existing boot medium first and mount NVMe for builds/state; move the root filesystem only after the baseline is reproducible. | Platform lead | Continue booting eMMC/SD while using NVMe as secondary storage. |
| R-034 | Production Android Auto rejects an independent receiver that lacks legitimate security provisioning. | High | Critical | Never copy shared credentials or bypass checks; retain error-7 evidence; develop through fake peers and Google's documented developer-mode DHU/ADB transport; investigate an official partner route. | Product/protocol/legal | Mark production wired/wireless projection blocked or limit releases to an explicit developer-mode research profile. |

## Review cadence

- Reassess R-001 through R-005 at every protocol milestone.
- Review the whole register at each milestone exit and release candidate.
- Any Critical-impact risk without an active mitigation owner blocks a release.
- Closed risks remain in history with the evidence and release in which they were closed.
